A private diary of your dictations

Sunoto will remember the text of each dictation on your Mac, so we can see where it goes wrong and prove that a fix helps. After each paste, the pill will also offer two buttons: Fix and Improve.

Step 1 built · text only, no audio in this version

In one minute

  • What is savedEach dictation's text at every stage, the app, and how long it took. One line per dictation in a daily file. No audio.
  • Off by defaultWe turn it on for ourselves first. Users get a choice once they can see their history.

What gets saved

Each stage of a dictation copies what it has into one entry. After the paste, the entry is written as one line.

Knowing what you meant

The diary shows what Sunoto produced. Three clues tell us what you wanted, and each is added as a short note on the entry:

  • You fix it: click Fix on the pill (below), or run sunoto-daemon history fix last "…". This records the right words, and it's the best clue.
  • You undo it: ⌘Z within 10 seconds of a paste (Mac only). Only ⌘Z is watched, and only its timing is kept.
  • You say it again within 20 seconds, in the same app.

No clue means “probably right”, never “proven right”.

Two buttons after each paste

For a few seconds after a paste, the pill shows Improve and Fix. They do different jobs:

  • Fix is for teaching. You type what you really said, and Sunoto saves it as a lesson. Your text in the app stays as it is.
  • Improve is for correcting. Sunoto fixes spelling and grammar and swaps the corrected text in, after you see it and press Replace.

What Improve runs on: our own small grammar model

Improve doesn't use Sunoto's AI editor, which needs a lot of memory, and it doesn't depend on Apple Intelligence, which many Macs don't have turned on. It uses a small model trained only to fix grammar, shipped with Sunoto:

  • Works everywhere. Every Mac Sunoto runs on, and later Linux, with the same results.
  • Small. About 60 times smaller than the AI editor's model. It loads when your pointer reaches the pill and unloads a minute later.
  • Ours to teach. Every Fix you save is a “wrong → right” example we can retrain it with.
  • Safe. Numbers, file names and links can't change, your word list (“PyTorch”, “Priya”) is put back if the model alters it, and big rewrites are refused.

Before building, we test it on about 50 real dictations from our own logs. We use it if most results get better, none change the meaning or any numbers, a warm call takes under half a second, and it uses under 300 MB while loaded. Otherwise we try the next, slightly larger model.

What improving looks like

A small analyzer inside Sunoto reads the diary on your Mac and produces three things. Each one leads to a change, and every change is checked against past dictations before it ships.

1 · Word-list suggestions, for you

Sunoto lists the words you keep fixing, and shows what each one would have changed in your past dictations. You choose which ones to add. Nothing is added on its own.

$ sunoto-daemon history suggest
1. priya   → Priya    fixed 6× · would change 15 past dictations: 6 match your fixes, 0 conflict
2. get hub → GitHub   fixed 3× · would change 4 past dictations: 3 match your fixes, 0 conflict
Add with: sunoto-daemon history suggest --accept 1,2

2 · A report, for the team

One local page, sunoto-daemon history report, shows speed, failures and fixes for each version side by side, and flags what needs a look. Until observability exists, this is our dashboard, built from our own Macs.

3 · A test set, for the team

Our own fixed dictations become test cases. A rule change ships only if sunoto-daemon eval shows it's better and it breaks nothing that was right. After that, the cases stay in the automatic tests for good. Comparing speech models needs audio, so that comes later.
